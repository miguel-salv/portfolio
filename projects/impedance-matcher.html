Automated Impedance Matcher

A precision automation tool designed to replace manual tuning in dynamic RF sputtering environments.

Embedded

4 months

CMU Hacker Fab

Visit Project

Overview

The CMU Hacker Fab is dedicated to democratizing nanofabrication by developing low-cost, open-source alternatives to expensive semiconductor manufacturing equipment. One of the core tools in the fab is the DIY RF Sputtering Chamber.

In RF sputtering, the chamber acts as a dynamic load with an impedance that fluctuates continuously as plasma conditions change throughout the deposition process. Traditionally, this requires an operator to constantly tend to the machine, manually tuning variable capacitors to minimize reflected power (Voltage Standing Wave Ratio, or VSWR). Commercial automated matching networks that solve this issue typically cost upwards of $3,000 to $8,000+, making them inaccessible for a low-cost student fab. To solve this, I designed and developed a custom automated RF matching network that eliminates this tedious manual intervention at a fraction of the cost.

System Overview

The automated matching network replaces manual knob-turning with high-precision digital control. Built around a Teensy 4.1 microcontroller and a custom T-network, the system intercepts forward and reverse power signals from the RF line. By feeding this into a gradient descent control algorithm, the system dynamically drives high-torque stepper motors attached to air-variable capacitors. This closed-loop system successfully minimizes reflected power, stabilizing the VSWR at ~1.2 and maintaining a reliable 95W forward power delivery to sustain a stable plasma for over an hour without human intervention.

PCB Design

I designed a custom 2-layer PCB that integrates the Teensy 4.1, dedicated motor drivers, and active cooling systems.

The board needed specific shielding and grounding techniques to suppress severe RF interference in the feedback loop, ensuring the microcontroller and motor drivers operate reliably in a high-noise environment.

Sensor Integration

In order to obtain the VSWR, I reverse-engineered a commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) SW-112 VSWR meter, carefully tapping into the internal toroidal sensing lines.

This was followed by an analog sensing pipeline utilizing the Teensy's ADCs to accurately digitize signals from high-impedance lines. I implemented buffering techniques to ensure that tapping these sensitive lines did not cause voltage sag or alter the RF characteristics of the system.

Firmware

I authored firmware to process the digitized forward and reverse power signals. Implemented a gradient descent control algorithm that intelligently calculates the optimal capacitor positions, driving the high-torque actuators to minimize VSWR in real-time.

Finally, I developed a local user interface featuring an I2C-driven OLED screen and a rotary encoder. This allows operators to easily toggle between automated and manual tuning modes while monitoring real-time power delivery and VSWR telemetry.

Results

This system successfully eliminated a highly tedious manual tuning step, significantly lowering the operational barrier for students running thin-film depositions. By completing the entire build for just $636, the project achieved an 80%+ cost reduction compared to $3000+ entry-level commercial alternatives and bypassed the need for expensive industrial units. The hardware and software were developed with open-source licensing in mind, allowing other Hacker Fabs globally to replicate this sputtering chamber setup.
